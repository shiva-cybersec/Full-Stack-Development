# FROM vs SINCE vs FOR (Complete Beginner Guide)

One of the biggest mistakes English learners make is confusing **From**, **Since**, and **For**.

The secret is simple:

- **From** = Starting point
- **Since** = Starting point + Still continuing
- **For** = Length of time

---

# Quick Comparison

| Word | Meaning | Roman Nepali | Answers Which Question? | Formula |
|------|---------|--------------|-------------------------|---------|
| **From** | Starting point | Bata (बाट) | Where/When did it start? | Start |
| **Since** | Starting point of an action that is still continuing | Dekhi (देखि) | Since when? | Start + Still Continuing |
| **For** | Length of time | Kati Samaya / Ko Lagi | How long? | Duration |

---

# 1. FROM

## Meaning

**From** tells us the **starting point** of a place, time, person, or event.

It **does NOT tell us** whether the action is finished or still continuing.

### Roman Nepali

**Bata (बाट)**

---

## Easy Formula

```
FROM = START
```

---

## Timeline

```
🏁---------------------------->

FROM
```

---

## Uses of FROM

### A. Starting Time

Examples

✅ School starts **from** Sunday.

✅ Office is open **from** 9 AM.

✅ The sale begins **from** tomorrow.

---

### B. Starting Place

Examples

✅ I came **from** Nepal.

✅ This bus goes **from** Kathmandu.

✅ She returned **from** Pokhara.

---

### C. Source

Examples

✅ I got this gift **from** my brother.

✅ I received a letter **from** my friend.

---

## FROM is usually used with

```
From → To

From → Until

From → Till
```

Examples

✅ From Monday to Friday

✅ From 9 AM until 5 PM

✅ From Nepal to India

---

# 2. SINCE

## Meaning

**Since** tells us the **starting point of an action that is STILL CONTINUING now.**

Roman Nepali

**Dekhi (देखि)**

---

## Easy Formula

```
SINCE = START + STILL CONTINUING
```

---

## Timeline

```
2020 ========================= NOW

SINCE
```

The action started in 2020 and is still happening.

---

## Examples

✅ I have lived here **since** 2020.

Started → 2020

Still living → YES

---

✅ She has worked here **since** Monday.

Started → Monday

Still working → YES

---

✅ We have been waiting **since** morning.

Started → Morning

Still waiting → YES

---

## Since is used with ONE POINT

Correct

✅ Since 2020

✅ Since Monday

✅ Since yesterday

✅ Since childhood

✅ Since morning

✅ Since lunch

✅ Since 6 PM

Notice

Every example is **one exact point in time.**

---

## Since usually uses Perfect Tense

Examples

✅ I have lived here since 2020.

✅ She has been studying since morning.

✅ They had lived there since childhood.

---

# 3. FOR

## Meaning

**For** tells us the **length of time (duration).**

Roman Nepali

**Kati Samaya / Ko Lagi**

---

## Easy Formula

```
FOR = DURATION
```

---

## Timeline

```

5 YEARS
```

FOR measures **how long** something lasts.

---

## Examples

✅ I have lived here **for** five years.

Question

How long?

Answer

Five years.

---

✅ She waited **for** two hours.

---

✅ We stayed there **for** a week.

---

## FOR uses DURATIONS

Correct

✅ For two hours

✅ For five days

✅ For three months

✅ For ten years

✅ For one week

✅ For a long time

Notice

Every example shows **length**, not an exact date.

### Mistake 1

---

### Mistake 2

❌ I have lived here for 2020.

Reason

✅ I have lived here since 2020.

---

❌ I live here since 2020.

Reason

Reason

Examples

---

### Question 2

Is it talking about **one exact point in time** and the action is **still continuing**?

Examples

- Since Monday
- Since 2020
- Since yesterday
- Since morning

✅ Use **SINCE**

---

### Question 3

Is it talking about **how long**?

Examples

- Two hours
- Five years
- One month
- Three weeks

✅ Use **FOR**

# Final Rule

> **FROM** starts a place, time, or range.
>
> **SINCE** starts an action that is still continuing.
>
> **FOR** tells the length of time.

# 1. Since vs For vs From

| Preposition | Simple Meaning | Roman Nepali | When to Use | When NOT to Use | Example |
|-------------|----------------|--------------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Since** | Shows the **starting point** of an action. | Dekhi (देखि) | Use when something started at a specific time and is **still continuing**. Mostly used with **has/have/had + V3** (Perfect Tense). | Don't use it to talk about **how long**. | I have lived here **since** 2020. |
| **For** | Shows **how long** something happens. | Ko lagi (को लागि) | Use for a period of time (2 hours, 5 days, 10 years). It can be used with many tenses. | Don't use it for a starting point like 2020 or Monday. | I have lived here **for** 5 years. |
| **From** | Shows the **starting point** of a time or place range. | Bata (बाट) | Use with **from...to...** or **from...until...** | Don't use it with Perfect Tense when talking about a fixed starting time. Use **since** instead. | He works **from** 9 AM **to** 5 PM. |

---

## Easy Difference

### Since = One Starting Point
Think about:
- Since Monday
- Since 2020
- Since morning
- Since my birthday

These are **specific points in time**.

✅ I have studied here **since** 2020.

---

### For = Time Length

Think about:
- For 2 hours
- For 5 days
- For 10 years
- For a month

These tell **how long**.

✅ She has waited **for** two hours.

---

### From = Start of a Range

Think about:

From Monday to Friday

From Nepal to India

From 9 AM to 5 PM

✅ School is open **from** Sunday **to** Friday.

---

## Memory Trick

**Since = Point**

```
2020 ●────────────► Now
```

**For = Duration**

```
██████████
5 years
```

**From = Start of Range**

```
From ----------- To
```

----------------------------

✅ Office is from 9 to 5.

# 2. Till / Until vs By

| Preposition | Simple Meaning | Roman Nepali | When to Use | When NOT to Use | Example |
|-------------|----------------|--------------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Till / Until** | Continue doing something **up to a time**. | Samma (सम्म) | Use when an action keeps happening until a certain time. | Don't use it for deadlines. | I will wait **until** 5 PM. |
| **By** | A deadline. The work must finish **before or at** that time. | Samma (Deadline) | Use for completing a task before a certain time. | Don't use it when the action continues the whole time. | Finish your homework **by** 5 PM. |

---

## Easy Difference

### Till / Until

The action **continues**.

Example:

```
Waiting
█████████████
1 PM      5 PM
```

✅ I will wait until 5 PM.

I keep waiting.

---

### By

The work finishes before the deadline.

```
Homework

✔ Done
3 PM

Deadline = 5 PM
```

It can finish at:

- 2 PM
- 3 PM
- 4 PM
- 5 PM

Not after 5 PM.

✅ Finish it by 5 PM.

---

## Memory Trick

Till = Keep doing.

By = Finish before deadline.

---

# Common Mistakes

❌ I will finish till 5 PM.

✅ I will finish by 5 PM.

------------------------

❌ I will wait by 5 PM.

✅ I will wait until 5 PM.

=====================================================

# 3. In vs Within vs Before vs After

| Word | Simple Meaning | Roman Nepali | When to Use | Time Meaning |
|------|----------------|--------------|-------------|--------------|
| **In** | Happens exactly when the time ends. | Ma / Bhitra | Use when something finishes after a certain amount of time. | Exactly at the end. |
| **Within** | Happens any time before the time ends. | Bhitra Bhitrai | Flexible deadline. | Before or at the end. |
| **Before** | Earlier than a time. | Agadi | Must happen earlier. | Strictly before. |
| **After** | Later than a time. | Pachhi | Happens later. | Strictly after. |

---

## Easy Difference

### In

Exactly after the full time.

Today

↓

1 Week

↓

Done

✅ I will finish it **in** one week.

---

### Within

Can finish anytime.

Day 1 ✅

Day 3 ✅

Day 5 ✅

Day 7 ✅

All are correct.

✅ Reply within one week.

---

### Before

Earlier only.

If meeting starts at 5 PM

You can come:

4:00 ✅

4:30 ✅

4:59 ✅

5:00 ❌

---

### After

Later only.

Meeting ends at 5 PM

Come:

5:01 ✅

6:00 ✅

Tomorrow ✅

---

## Memory Trick

In = Exactly after the period.

Within = Anytime before the period ends.

Before = Earlier.

After = Later.

# 1. Over vs Above | Under vs Below

These four words confuse many students.

The easiest way to remember them is:

👉 **Over / Under = Direct Position**
👉 **Above / Below = Higher or Lower (Not necessarily direct)**

---

# Over vs Above

| Word | Simple Meaning | Roman Nepali | When to Use | When NOT to Use | Example |
|------|----------------|--------------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Over** | Directly above something. Sometimes touching or covering it. It also means **more than**. | Sidhai Mathi (सिधै माथि) | Use when one thing is directly above another or covering it. Also use for numbers (more than). | Don't use it if something is only higher but not directly above. | The fan is **over** my head. |
| **Above** | Higher than something but not directly over it. | Mathi (माथि) | Use when talking about height only. | Don't use it if something is directly covering another thing. | The airplane is **above** the clouds. |

---

## Easy Difference

### Over

One thing is directly above another.

```

🙂
```

The umbrella is **over** my head.

---

Another example

```
Blanket

```

The blanket is **over** the person.

---

### Above

Only higher.

```
Bird

        🐦



Tree

🌳
```

The bird is above the tree.

It is higher.

It is NOT directly over the tree.

---

## Another Meaning of Over

Over also means **more than**.

Examples

Over 18 years old

Temperature over 35°C

---

## Memory Trick

**Over**

✔ Directly above

✔ Covering

✔ More than

| **Below** | Lower than something. Not necessarily directly under it. | Tala (तल) | Use when comparing levels or height. | Don't use it for direct position. | The village is **below** the mountain. |

# PART 3 — OTHER CONFUSING PREPOSITIONS

This section explains some prepositions that look similar but have different meanings.

======================================================

# 1. On vs Upon

Many students think these are different.

Actually, they have **almost the same meaning**.

The only difference is:

✅ **On** = Common English

✅ **Upon** = Formal English

---

| Word | Simple Meaning | Roman Nepali | When to Use | When NOT to Use | Example |
|------|----------------|--------------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **On** | Touching the surface of something. | Mathi (माथि) | Everyday speaking and writing. | None. It is the normal word. | The book is **on** the table. |
| **Upon** | Same meaning as **on**, but more formal. It is also used when something suddenly moves onto another thing. | Mathi (Formal) | Books, stories, speeches, formal writing. | Don't use it in normal conversation. It sounds too formal. | The cat jumped **upon** the wall. |

---

## Easy Difference

### On

Used in daily life.

Examples

The phone is **on** the table.

The picture is **on** the wall.

He sat **on** the chair.

---

### Upon

Used in formal English.

Examples

The king looked **upon** his people.

The bird landed **upon** the tree.

Once **upon** a time...

---

## Memory Trick

ON

↓

Daily English

UPON

↓

Formal English

---

# Common Mistakes

❌ Once on a time.

✅ Once upon a time.

----------------------------

❌ The pen is upon the table.

✅ The pen is on the table.

(The second one sounds much more natural.)

======================================================

# 2. Beside vs Besides

Many students confuse these because they look almost the same.

Their meanings are completely different.

---

| Word | Simple Meaning | Roman Nepali | When to Use | When NOT to Use | Example |
|------|----------------|--------------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **Beside** | Next to something. | Chheu Ma (छेउमा) | Physical position. | Don't use it to mean "also". | Ram is sitting **beside** me. |
| **Besides** | Also, in addition to. | Bahek / Sathai (बाहेक / साथै) | Adding more information. | Don't use it for physical position. | **Besides** English, she speaks Nepali. |

---

## Easy Difference

### Beside


Ram is beside Hari.

---

### Besides

Means "also."

Examples

Besides English,

I know Nepali.

Besides football,

I also play cricket.

---

## Memory Trick

Beside

↓

Next to

Besides

↓

Also

✅ Beside me is my brother.

----------------------------

❌ Beside English, I know Nepali.

✅ Besides English, I know Nepali.

# 3. By vs With

These are very important.

Many exam questions come from these two.

---

| Word | Simple Meaning | Roman Nepali | When to Use | When NOT to Use | Example |
|------|----------------|--------------|-------------|-----------------|---------|
| **By** | Shows who did the action. | Dwara / Le (द्वारा / ले) | Passive voice. | Don't use it for tools. | The letter was written **by** Ram. |
| **With** | Shows the tool or object used. | Sanga / Prayog Garera (सँग / प्रयोग गरेर) | Tool, instrument or object. | Don't use it for the person who did the action. | He cut the apple **with** a knife. |

---

## Easy Difference

### By

Answers

Example

The picture was painted

↓

by Rita.

---

### With

Answers

How?

Using what?

Example

He wrote the letter

↓

with a pen.

---

## Use Both Together

Example

He was stabbed

**by** the thief

**with** a knife.

Who?

↓

The thief.

Tool?

↓

Knife.

---

## More Examples

by my mother.

The cake was cut

with a knife.

----------------------------

The homework was completed

by the students.

They wrote it

with a pencil.

# 1. TIME PREPOSITIONS

## Since vs For vs From

| Word | Remember |
|------|----------|

| From | Start of a range |

From Sunday to Friday

## Till / Until vs By

Till / Until

↓

Action continues.

Example

I will wait until 5 PM.

----------------------

By

↓

Deadline

Example

Finish your homework by 5 PM.

In

↓

Exactly after the time finishes.

Example

I'll finish in one week.

----------------------

Within

↓

Anytime before the deadline.

Example

Reply within one week.

----------------------

Before

↓

Earlier than.

Example

Come before 5 PM.

----------------------

After

↓

Later than.

Example

Come after lunch.

Exact Time

----------------------

↓

on my birthday

----------------------

In

↓

in winter
